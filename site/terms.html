The numbers are estimates, not a bill

Notchmeter is not a billing record, and it is not authorised by any AI vendor. Nothing it shows is an authoritative statement of what you have used, what remains, or what you owe. Do not make a billing decision on the strength of it.

The app shows two kinds of figure, and both are estimates.

  • Readings borrowed from each vendor. For Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Antigravity / Gemini CLI and GitHub Copilot, the app makes the same read-only status request that vendor's own tool or dashboard makes, using the login that tool already keeps on your Mac. Three tools have a second, more detailed read behind a switch in Settings, and where that switch is on the app makes that request too. Cursor's is on unless you switch it off, because it reads the same account the summary already reads; the Codex and Copilot ones reach endpoints of their own and stay off until you turn them on. The base rate is one read every five minutes per tool. It can happen sooner when you hover a card, press Command-R, wake or unlock the Mac, or when an assistant hook fires, and it slows or stops when nobody is looking: reads pause while the Mac is asleep, the screen is locked or the displays are off, and come less often on battery or after a long idle. So a figure can be seconds old or a good deal older. Between reads the app holds the last good reading. Every figure is only ever as complete and as correct as the answer the vendor gave, and it can be stale, or missing while you are offline.
  • Cost worked out on your Mac. The cost card is calculated locally from your own Claude Code and Codex transcripts, priced against a table of published API list prices that ships with the app, plus any pricing table you or Claude Code have set locally. If a vendor changes its prices, or if your plan is not billed at list prices, that figure will not match what you are actually charged.

Pace ticks, projections, run-out times and the advice lines are forecasts drawn from recent behaviour. They describe what would happen if you carried on as you have been. They are not promises, and they are frequently wrong when your day changes shape.

So do not rely on Notchmeter for billing, budgeting, expense claims, chargebacks, contractual commitments or anything else where being wrong costs you something. For any of that, check the vendor's own dashboard or invoice. If a vendor changes or withdraws the endpoint a meter reads, that meter reports an error and waits, or falls back to something already on your Mac, as the Codex meter does with the session files Codex writes locally. It does not go looking for another way in over the network, and in particular it never makes an inference request to get at a figure.

Other people's services, and your own accounts

Parts of this work only because other companies' services are involved. Each of them is run by someone else, under their own terms and their own privacy policy, and we do not control them:

  • The website is hosted by Vercel, which serves every byte of it and keeps standard server request logs. The site itself runs no analytics and sets no cookies.
  • Downloads are served by GitHub, both the DMG and the Homebrew tap.
  • Update checks in the installed app go to GitHub, about once a day by default, and can be switched off in Settings. GitHub sees that request, including the IP address it came from. It is the only request the app itself makes over the internet that is not a usage reading, and it goes to GitHub rather than to us.
  • Usage readings in the app go to each assistant vendor's own usage endpoint over HTTPS, using the login that vendor's own tool already keeps on your Mac.
  • The optional donation is handled by Stripe on Stripe's own pages. We never see your card details.

What the site and the app do with data is set out in the Privacy Policy.

Your accounts with the AI vendors are yours, and your agreements with them are between you and them. You are responsible for using Notchmeter in a way that is consistent with those agreements. As the project's README says plainly: each vendor's terms are written for its own apps and restrict automated access in broad language, and reading an app's saved login from outside that app is a grey area under each of them. Notchmeter stays on the narrowest path it can, but whether to run it under your account is your decision, and the consequences with that vendor are yours.
