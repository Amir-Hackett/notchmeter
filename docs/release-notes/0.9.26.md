# Notchmeter 0.9.26

### Full-screen apps

- **The readouts step aside for VLC.** VLC's full screen (⌘F, with *Use the native fullscreen mode* off, which is how VLC 3.0 ships) is not a macOS full-screen Space: it stretches a window over the desktop and hides the Dock and the menu bar itself, and every other window stays on screen behind it. Notchmeter's rule asked that the covering app be the only one on screen, which that never is, so the rings stayed over the film with *Show over full-screen apps* off. The rule now also calls a display full-screen when a window covers it and the app has taken both the Dock's and the menu bar's windows away, which is what that mode and most games look like. Moving the pointer to the top edge brings the menu bar back, and with other apps' windows on screen behind the player the readouts come back with it; both go again once the bar has. Because that mode announces nothing to other apps, Notchmeter now also keeps looking while a window the display's width is on screen, which a player's is once it has fitted itself to the video; a player whose window is narrower than that is caught at the next app switch.

### Cost

- **Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 are priced, and Haiku 3.5 is retired.** Both models reached Anthropic's pricing page after the 24 September snapshot, so a Sonnet 5.5 transcript line was priced as Sonnet 5 (right by chance on input and output, wrong on cache reads, which Sonnet 5.5 bills at 0.05x) and a Haiku 5.5 line at the family's newest row, Haiku 4.5, ten times its base rate. That base rate is the only one applied: Haiku 5.5's higher rate for prompts over 100,000 tokens is not modelled, so a request that large reads low. Both have rows of their own now, in the build and in the catalog. Haiku 3.5 left the page the same week and is kept at its last published rate for old transcripts. The weekly pricing check now also reads every priced model row on the page and fails on one the snapshot does not know, which is how these two went unnoticed from their launches on 28 September and 7 October.
- **Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 are priced.** Both launched on 9 November at $8 and $40 per million tokens with a $0.20 cache read, and had no rows of their own, so their transcript lines were priced as Fable 5 and Mythos 5: a quarter too high on input and output, and five times too high on every cache read. Both have rows now, fast mode's $16 and $80 included, in the build and in the catalog, and the snapshot is dated 18 November. An unknown Fable or Mythos version falls back to this row, the family's newest.

### Sessions

- **Dropping a file on the notch works.** 0.9.25 said it did, and it never did: inside the menu bar's band, the top 33 points of the display, macOS offers a drag only to windows at the menu bar's own level, the way a status item takes a drop, and the notch panel sits above that level so it can draw over the bar. A file dragged onto the rings fell straight through to whatever window lay behind them. A clear window at the bar's level now lies under the collapsed notch and takes the drag: the panel opens as the file comes over the rings, and the drop lands whether you let go on the rings, on the notch or on the panel below.

### Under the hood

- **Sparkle 2.10.1.** The updater framework moves from 2.9.6 to 2.10.1 (released 2026-10-25), which fixes delta updates re-applying file system compression on macOS 27 and an update installing early from a path in NFC-normalised Unicode. It requires macOS 12; Notchmeter requires 15.
